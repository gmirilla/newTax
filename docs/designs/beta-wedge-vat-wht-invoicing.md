# Design: NaijaBooks Beta Wedge — VAT/WHT-Correct Invoicing Over Tax-Law Fear

Generated by /office-hours on 2026-11-10
Branch: primarydev
Repo: gmirilla/newTax
Status: APPROVED
Mode: Startup

## Problem Statement

NaijaBooks (production: accounttaxng.com; beta mirror: laravelbuildlab.com) opened
its beta program yesterday with zero enrollees. The founder's working theory for
why people would pay was fear of Nigeria's new tax law (Nigeria Tax Act 2025, in
force Jan 1 2026). That framing doesn't survive contact with the founder's own
named users: both are solo, 3-5-invoice/month side businesses, almost certainly
under every turnover threshold in the new Act, and small-enterprise e-invoicing
enforcement is reported (unconfirmed — see Constraints) to not start until
mid-2027. One of the two named users explicitly disbelieves the law will be
enforced on him — and on the figures found so far, he may well be right. The
real, already-live pain the founder identified under pressure was
narrower and more concrete: VAT/WHT reconciliation errors on invoices (a client
withholds tax, the founder also charges/remits VAT, and the two don't reconcile
cleanly when FIRS/NRS reviews it for a Tax Clearance Certificate).

## Demand Evidence

One confirmed, specific, acute incident: the founder personally had a client
withhold WHT on an invoice, also paid/charged VAT on that invoice, and had
difficulty explaining the resulting mismatch to FIRS when seeking his Tax
Clearance Certificate (TCC). This is mechanically corroborated by current
Nigerian e-invoicing documentation: buyer TIN mismatches cause invoice rejection,
invoices require FIRS/NRS validation before a buyer can claim input VAT credit,
and VAT is payable on receipt of payment rather than at invoice date — a
structural timing gap that produces exactly this kind of reconciliation mismatch.

"Several businesses I know have this issue" was also asserted. This is plausible
given the mechanics above but is **not yet named evidence** — it is the single
biggest gap in this design, and closing it is the Assignment below.

## Status Quo

Two named solo operators:
- The founder himself (~3 invoices/month)
- A friend (~5 invoices/month), who does not believe the new law will be
  enforced against a business his size — plausible, per the unconfirmed
  enforcement-timeline figures in Constraints below

Before anything else, both ran their invoicing informally: non-sequential
invoice numbers, unprofessional-looking invoices, lost track of who had paid,
and haphazard profit/expense records. On top of that baseline mess, the founder
hit the specific WHT/VAT reconciliation failure described above when a client
withheld tax on an invoice he'd also charged VAT on.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

Not "Nigerian SMEs." Specifically: very small or solo invoicing businesses,
starting as low as 3-5 invoices/month, who have been or will be caught by a
WHT/VAT mismatch when a client withholds tax on an invoice and it doesn't
reconcile cleanly for their Tax Clearance Certificate.

Narrowest wedge: a "Clean Invoice" capability inside the existing beta —
automatic, correct VAT computation plus a manually-set WHT rate on every
invoice (see Approach A below for why WHT isn't automatic in V1), plus a
standing reconciliation record the user can produce if FIRS/NRS ever
questions it.

But the *actual* narrowest next step is not more engineering — it's
non-technical: personally walking 2-3 more known businesses through the VAT
computation feature that already exists in the codebase, to convert "several
businesses have this issue" from an assertion into named, specific evidence.

## Constraints

- Pre-paying-customer stage: beta opened yesterday, zero enrollees.
- The founder is one of the two named users — real signal, but a known bias
  (self-validation isn't external validation).
- Much of the needed computation logic already exists: git history shows a VAT
  computation page with transaction review, input-VAT handling, and GL-posted
  invoicing already shipped (`feat: enhance VAT computation page with
  transaction review and CSV export functionality`, `feat: enhance VAT payment
  processing with transaction tracking and input VAT handling`).
- Nigeria's 2025/2026 tax reform has genuinely conflicting published guidance:
  sources disagree on the small-company turnover exemption (₦50M vs ₦100M) and
  the VAT registration threshold (₦25M vs ₦50M). Any customer-facing copy citing
  a specific figure must be confirmed against official NRS/FIRS text first —
  this design doc does not treat either figure as settled.
- The "mid-2027 small-enterprise e-invoicing enforcement" claim used in the
  Problem Statement and Status Quo above comes from secondary compliance-vendor
  sources (e.g. vatcalc.com, beancount.io), not verified against official
  NRS/FIRS text or a primary legal source. It is treated with the same
  confidence level as the disputed thresholds above — plausible, not settled —
  and should be re-confirmed before being used to tell a real prospect "you
  don't need to worry yet."
- The codebase's existing WHT logic (`WhtService::isExemptFromWht()`,
  `app/Services/WhtService.php`) checks tenant turnover against
  `CitService::SMALL_COMPANY_THRESHOLD`, hardcoded to ₦50,000,000
  (`app/Services/CitService.php`). This exemption check is invoked only on
  the payable side (see the next bullet) — it does not run during the
  invoice walkthrough in the Assignment below. The ₦50M figure only matters
  if the walkthrough conversation separately turns to whether the
  participant's business is CIT/WHT-exempt at their turnover level at all,
  not for the WHT rate/amount shown on the invoice itself.
- `WhtService`'s rate table and exemption check currently run only on the
  payable side (expenses/vendors) — invoice creation
  (`InvoiceController`, `app/Http/Controllers/InvoiceController.php`) takes
  `wht_rate` directly from request input (defaulting to a flat 5%) with no
  transaction-type, recipient-type, or exemption logic applied. "Automatic,
  correct WHT on invoices" does not exist yet; VAT computation on invoices
  does.

## Premises

1. The real problem isn't "fear of the new tax law" (too broad, and doesn't
   apply to the founder's own 3-5-invoice/month segment) — it's **VAT/WHT-correct
   invoicing plus payment-status reconciliation that holds up when FIRS/NRS
   checks it.** — Agreed.
2. There is exactly one confirmed, acute, named incident of this pain (the
   founder's own), plus an unconfirmed "several businesses I know have this
   too" — real signal, but not yet a second or third name. — Agreed.
3. A meaningful amount of the technical capability this needs may already
   exist in the codebase (VAT computation, invoice GL posting, input-VAT
   handling) — this may be more a positioning/packaging gap than an
   engineering gap. — Agreed.
4. Zero people are using the product yet (beta opened yesterday, no
   enrollees) — the next real evidence has to come from enrolling and
   observing the people already named, not from building more first. — Agreed.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Minimal viable — "Clean Invoice" feature inside the existing beta
Ship one narrow, visible feature in the current beta: on invoice creation, the
app computes correct VAT and produces a reconciliation record the user can hand
to FIRS/NRS if ever questioned. **V1 scope correction (found during review):**
VAT computation on invoices already exists and can genuinely ship fast — but
automatic WHT determination on invoices does not exist yet (see Constraints:
`WhtService`'s rate table/exemption logic only runs on the payable side today).
V1 should let the user pick/edit the WHT rate manually against the VAT-correct
invoice, not claim automatic WHT; porting `WhtService`'s rate table and
exemption check to invoice creation is real, separate follow-on work.
- Effort: S for VAT-correct invoicing + manual WHT entry; +S/M follow-on to
  port automatic WHT exemption/rate logic to the receivable side | Risk: Low
- Pros: VAT side genuinely reuses existing code and ships within days; gives
  the beta cohort a concrete reason to enroll beyond "try our app."
- Cons: still unproven whether anyone will pay for it specifically; doesn't by
  itself produce new named evidence; "fully automatic WHT" is not V1.
- Reuses: existing VAT computation page, invoice GL posting, input-VAT
  handling. Does NOT yet reuse `WhtService`'s rate/exemption logic for
  invoices — that's net-new wiring, not reuse.

### Approach B: Ideal architecture — reposition the whole product around "compliance-safe invoicing"
Make VAT/WHT-correct, FIRS-defensible invoicing the flagship story for the
entire platform: TIN validation on invoice creation, a payment-status-aware VAT
ledger, automatic WHT credit-note reconciliation, built to extend as
e-invoicing enforcement rolls downmarket through 2027.
- Effort: L/XL | Risk: Medium-High
- Pros: most defensible long-term position; rides a real regulatory tailwind.
- Cons: commits real engineering time before a single paying or even
  confirmed-enrolled customer exists — the "build the full platform before
  anyone can use it" trap the diagnostic warns against.
- Reuses: current VAT/invoice/GL code as a foundation, but needs substantially
  more (TIN-validation integration, credit-note workflow).

### Approach C: Creative/lateral — use the existing tool as a free evidence-gathering move before building anything new
Before writing new code, personally walk the "several businesses" mentioned
through the already-built VAT computation/invoice-review feature, for free,
surfacing their actual WHT/VAT mismatches in their own numbers.
- Effort: S (near-zero new code — outreach/process work) | Risk: Low
- Pros: directly fixes the single biggest gap in the evidence (everything so
  far is n=1); costs nothing to try; the result tells you whether A or B is
  worth building next.
- Cons: feels like "not building" to a founder who wants to ship; requires
  personal outreach, not engineering.

## Recommended Approach

**C, then A.** This isn't a product problem right now — the VAT computation
capability already exists. It's an evidence problem: one confirmed data point
(the founder) and a vague "several businesses" that hasn't been turned into
names. Approach C costs nothing and directly produces the missing evidence;
Approach A is the natural, cheap next step once C gives 2-3 real reactions to
build from. Approach B is a bet that isn't warranted yet — it spends real
engineering time on a positioning only one person has actually validated.

## Open Questions

- The exact small-company CIT exemption threshold (₦50M vs ₦100M) and VAT
  registration threshold (₦25M vs ₦50M) are disputed across sources and must be
  confirmed against official NRS/FIRS text before appearing in any
  customer-facing copy.
- Whether "several businesses I know have this issue" survives contact with
  actual named people once walked through the existing tool.
- Whether WHT/VAT reconciliation pain is common below the ₦25-50M threshold
  generally, or concentrated specifically in businesses dealing with corporate
  clients who withhold tax (which may itself be the real targeting signal,
  more than raw invoice volume).

## Success Criteria

- Within 2 weeks (Approach C): at least 2 of the "several businesses" named
  and have given a specific, observed reaction to seeing their own VAT/WHT
  numbers in the existing tool (not a survey answer).
- Within that same 2-week window: at least 1 of them states willingness to
  pay — not "interesting," an actual behavior: asks to keep using it, asks
  what it costs, or gets upset when told it might go away.
- Within 4 weeks, after Approach C's evidence comes in (Approach A): the
  Clean Invoice reconciliation record is live in the beta and has been used
  on at least 3 real invoices from real users.

## Dependencies

None new. Approach A's VAT-side ships by reusing existing VAT computation/
invoice GL code; its WHT side needs new wiring (see Approach A's V1 scope
correction above), not pure reuse. Approach C requires no engineering at all —
only the founder's own outreach time.

Before running Approach C's walkthroughs: tell each participant up front (1)
this is an unreleased beta, not a finished product, and (2) their real invoice
and financial figures entered during the session are retained in the beta
database like any other tenant's data — get their verbal OK before using real
numbers, or use anonymized/round-number test figures if they'd rather not.

## The Assignment

This week: sit down with your friend and at least one other business you know,
walk them through the existing VAT computation/invoice-review feature in the
live beta using their own real invoice numbers (after the consent/data note
in Dependencies above), and write down exactly what they say the moment they
see their own WHT/VAT mismatch — not whether they say the idea is interesting.

Tell them plainly what they're actually looking at: the WHT rate/amount shown
on an invoice today is a manual, user-entered figure (flat 5% by default) —
**not** an automatic exemption or transaction-type determination (that logic
exists in the codebase today only on the expense/payable side, not for
invoices — see Constraints). Don't let a participant mistake a manually-set
number for a verified tax result. Separately — and only if the conversation
gets into whether they're exempt from CIT/WHT at their turnover level at all —
note that the ₦50M small-company threshold behind that separate question is
itself unconfirmed against official NRS/FIRS text (see Constraints).

## What I noticed about how you think

- When I handed you a "fear of the new law" framing that fit your own anxiety,
  you didn't just take it — you asked me to actually check it against the real
  law and real Nigerian business behavior. Most people take validation that
  flatters them and move on; you asked for the harder answer.
- When I pushed back that "easy bookkeeping and invoices" was too vague, you
  didn't retreat into more generalities — you immediately produced five
  concrete, specific failures, one of which had a real institutional
  consequence (your Tax Clearance Certificate). That's a founder's actual
  experience showing up in real time, not a pitch.
- You named yourself as one of your only two users without being asked to
  obscure it. Most founders reach for "customers" language to sound more
  validated than they are; you didn't.
